The Last Order

Rosie's Diner stayed open until two, and in the summer of 1952 Dot Mancuso worked every one of those last hours. The neon over the counter buzzed like a trapped bee.

At half past midnight a man in a wet hat took the corner booth and ordered pie he never touched. He came back the next night, and the next.

"Cherry again?" Dot asked. "Whatever you'd recommend," he said, and for the first time he smiled.

She learned his name was Walter. He played piano at the hotel on Main Street and hated the songs they made him play.

On the fourth night it rained so hard the street outside turned to glass. The streetlights swam in it.

Walter stood under the shop awning across the road with no umbrella, waiting for the last bus.

Dot locked the till, turned off the neon, and walked out into the rain with the diner's big striped umbrella.

"You'll catch your death," she said. "Then I'd better catch the bus with company," he said.

They missed it. They walked the whole length of Main Street instead, past dark shop windows and humming lamps.

Years later the diner was gone, but she kept the umbrella by the door, and he still ordered cherry.

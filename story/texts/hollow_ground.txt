Hollow Ground

The cabin had been empty since the Harlan family left in a hurry in the autumn of 1987. Nell bought it for almost nothing and drove up through the forest at dusk.

The pines closed over the road like a held breath. When she cut the engine, nothing sang. Not a bird. Not an insect.

Inside, the table was still set for four. Dust lay on the plates in a perfect grey snow.

That night something knocked on the door three times. Nell opened it. There was no one there, only the dark trees and a path of small footprints leading away.

She followed them at first light. They ended at the old graveyard behind the ridge, among leaning headstones and a rusted iron fence.

Four new stones stood in the front row. The names were Harlan. The dates were next year.

"You shouldn't be here," whispered a voice behind the fence. When she turned, the graveyard was empty.

Back at the cabin the basement door stood open. It had been nailed shut when she arrived.

Down there, beside the old boiler and the shelves of jars, someone had chalked a fifth name on the wall.

It was hers.

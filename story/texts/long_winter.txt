The Long Winter

In the winter of 1917 the snow came early to the Argonne, and Private Tomas Weller learned to measure time in frozen boots.

The field beyond the wire was white and perfectly quiet, the kind of quiet that made men whisper.

At dusk the guns began again. The trench shook, and the sandbags bled snow onto the duckboards.

"Keep your head down, Weller," said the sergeant. "Keep it down and you'll see Christmas."

A shell landed short. When Tomas woke he was in the field hospital ward, under a grey blanket, between rows of iron beds.

A nurse with tired eyes wrote his name on a board by the window. Outside, the snow kept falling on the lorries.

He wrote a letter home on the back of a ration card. He told his mother the food was good. He told her it had stopped snowing.

On Christmas Eve someone found a candle and set it on the ward's long table. Nobody sang, but nobody slept either.

In the morning the guns were silent. Far across the snowfield, a single crow walked along the wire.

"Is it over?" Tomas asked. "For today," said the nurse.

The Night Shift

The rain had not stopped for three days, and the city's neon signs bled into every puddle on Mercer Street. Detective Ines Calloway stood at the mouth of the alley, collar up, listening to the steam hiss from the grates.

A noodle stall was still open at the far end. Its lanterns swayed over an empty counter. The vendor had vanished, but his radio still played, tinny and far away.

"You're late," said a voice from somewhere above the fire escape. Ines did not look up. "I'm always late," she said.

She found the envelope taped under the vending machine, exactly where the caller had promised. Inside was a key and a photograph of a kitchen she knew too well.

By morning she was standing in that kitchen. Her mother's kitchen. The kettle was still warm on the stove.

On the cork board, between the shopping lists and the old postcards, someone had pinned a new note. It said only: Go to the lighthouse.

She drove to the coast at dusk. The island sat black against the sunset, the lighthouse already turning.

The keeper's cottage door was open. The lantern above swept the sea in long, patient circles.

On the cliff edge she finally understood. Someone had been leading her here for years, one clue at a time.

"Hello, Ines," said a voice behind her. Far out on the horizon, a boat's light blinked once and went dark.

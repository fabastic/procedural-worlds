The Dragon's Tithe

Every hundred years the village of Harrowmere paid the dragon its tithe, and this year the lot fell to Wren, the miller's youngest.

At dawn the market was silent. The stalls stood empty, the cobbles wet with dew, and no one would meet her eye.

That night the tavern was full and no one drank. The innkeeper set a mug of mead before her by the fire and said nothing at all.

"They say it eats the gold and spits out the bones," muttered an old man by the hearth. "They say a lot of things," said Wren.

Before sunrise she climbed the long road to the castle, where the old king kept the tithe in an iron chest.

The great hall was cold. Banners hung in tatters between the pillars, and the throne sat empty on its dais.

There was no king. There had been no king for fifty years. The chest at the foot of the throne held nothing but a map.

The map led to the ruins on the moor, where broken stones stood in a ring like the teeth of something buried.

She waited there through the dusk with nothing in her hands. The wind came first, then the shadow, then the heat.

"No gold?" asked the dragon. "Only a question," said Wren. "Why do you keep coming back?"

The dragon was quiet for a long time. "Because no one ever asked," it said, and lay down among the stones to tell her.
